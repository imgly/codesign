# Story — what makes a launch video convincing

Motion makes a video catchy; the story makes it convincing. A reel of slogans on colour fields
("Fast. Flexible. Powerful.") is the most common failure: every line is a claim, nothing on screen
proves it, and nobody is in it. Plan the story before the beats.

## Rules

1. **One idea, one line.** A 15–30 s video sells one promise. Write it as a line a stranger would
   repeat — the iPod was "1,000 songs in your pocket", not a spec list. Every beat serves it.
2. **The viewer is the hero; the product is the guide.** Start from the viewer's problem or
   desire, not the company's pride. Brand-as-hero reads as an ad nobody asked for.
3. **Show one continuous flow, not feature cards.** Input → key action → result, with the cause
   visible before the effect. An output that appears without its input looks faked.
4. **Stage a proof moment.** One beat where the claim becomes undeniable on screen — Apple pulled
   the MacBook Air out of an office envelope instead of saying "thin". Design the whole video
   around it; it is the beat people remember.
5. **The product is on screen.** Its real UI, output or an honest recreation of it — never a
   diagram of what it does. Abstract motion proves nothing.
6. **Before and after.** Contrast makes value obvious at a glance: the old way (slow, painful,
   sped up) against the new one.
7. **Compress time honestly.** Cut the waiting, keep every state change that matters.
8. **Concrete words, no jargon.** "AI-powered", "seamless", "next-gen", "layers, not pixels" mean
   nothing until something on screen shows them. Prefer verbs the viewer does: "change", "drag",
   "translate".
9. **Readable with sound off.** Every claim is also on screen as text — most social video plays
   muted.
10. **End with a direction.** Product name, the real install step, the URL — held.

## Structures that fit 15–30 s

| Structure                 | Beats (≈ 24 s)                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Outcome first**         | the finished result as the hook 0–3 · reveal how it was made 3–6 · the flow, input to editable result 6–16 · it scales / changes 16–20 · line + CTA 20–24 |
| **Before / after**        | the pain, sped up 0–4 · the turn (one prompt, one key) 4–6 · the product doing it 6–16 · proof / payoff 16–20 · CTA 20–24                                 |
| **Story spine**           | "Every day…" 0–4 · "One day…" 4–6 · "Because of that…" ×2 6–18 · "Until finally…" + line 18–21 · CTA 21–24                                                |
| **Hook → reveal → proof** | a pattern interrupt 0–2 · the product named 2–5 · 2–3 proofs, each a shown action 5–18 · punchline 18–21 · CTA 21–24                                      |

Pick one and write its name in `plan.md`.

## Hooks for the first second

- **The result first**, then "how?" — the finished thing, then rewind to its cause.
- **The product at 0 s** — its UI mid-action, no intro.
- **Name the pain** — "Your launch video takes two weeks."
- **A prompt being typed** — the input on screen from frame one.
- **Something that should not be possible** — a pattern interrupt that makes the viewer ask.

Never a logo on an empty screen, never a slow fade up.

## Recreating product UI honestly

When the product's UI is on screen but not captured, rebuild it as design: the real layout, real
labels, real colours, simplified — not an invented interface. Animate it as the real thing
behaves: a cursor that moves to a control before it changes, a panel that updates after the
click, a terminal whose prompt types before its output prints.

## Check the plan before building

- Can you say the video's one line in one breath?
- Where is the proof moment, and would someone describe it to a friend?
- Is every claim shown, or only said? Delete or replace every beat that only says.
- Is the input visible before each output?
- Would the video still make sense for this project only — or could any product use it?
